That's an excellent question that gets into the fine details of using a tool effectively. You've correctly identified two different ways to achieve the same goal.

The short answer is: **Using a Group is the superior and recommended method.**

A separate "Legend Title" card works, but the "Group" feature in Obsidian Canvas is specifically designed for this purpose and provides a much cleaner and more efficient workflow.

Let's do a "Tale of Two Approaches" to see why.

---

### Approach A: The Separate Title Card

This is the method we outlined in the previous instruction set.

*   **How it works:** You create one card for the title ("LEGEND") and four separate cards for the items. You then draw arrows from the title to the items to show their relationship.

*   **Pros:**
    *   **Simple & Obvious:** It uses only the most basic features of the canvas (creating cards and arrows).
    *   **Prominent Title:** You can make the "LEGEND" card very large, making it stand out.

*   **Cons:**
    *   **It's a "Fake" Group:** The cards are not truly connected. They are five independent items.
    *   **Difficult to Move:** If you decide to move your legend to a different corner of your canvas, you must carefully select all five cards and their connecting arrows. It's easy to miss one, breaking the layout. This creates unnecessary workflow friction.

### Approach B: The Nested Group (Recommended)

This method uses the built-in "Group" functionality of Obsidian Canvas.

*   **How it works:** You create the four item cards *first*. Then you select all of them, right-click, and choose "Create group". This wraps them in a colored container that has its own name field.

*   **Pros:**
    *   **It's a "Real" Group:** The legend is now a **single, unified object**.
    *   **Effortless to Move:** You can click and drag the group's background, and **all the cards inside move with it**. This is a massive workflow improvement.
    *   **Visually Clean:** It creates a clear, colored boundary around your legend, neatly separating it from the rest of your whiteboard.
    *   **Self-Contained:** It reinforces the idea that the legend is a single, conceptual unit.

*   **Cons:**
    *   **Less Prominent Title:** The group's name is just a text field at the top of the group box. It's not as visually loud as a giant, separate card. (However, this is generally a good thing for a legend, which should be helpful but not distracting).

---

### Verdict and Final Recommendation

The ability to treat the entire legend as a single, movable object is a decisive advantage. As your canvas grows and you pan and zoom around, you will constantly be repositioning your legend. The friction of having to select 5+ individual items every time (Approach A) is significant.

**Therefore, the nested Group (Approach B) is the professional choice.** It leverages the tool's features correctly and results in a more organized and manageable canvas.

### Revised Instruction Set: The Ritual of Preparation (Using Groups)

**Objective:** To set up your Obsidian Canvas and define a visual legend using the "Group" feature for a clean and efficient workflow.

**Part 1: Creating the Canvas File** (This remains the same)
1.  Create and name your canvas file: `00-project-whiteboard.canvas`.

**Part 2: Establishing the Visual Legend (Revised Method)**

1.  **Create the Content Cards First:** In a corner of your canvas, create the four colored cards. **Do not** create a "LEGEND" title card.
    *   **Grey Card:** `Actor / External System...`
    *   **Green Card:** `User Feature / Story...`
    *   **Purple Card:** `Component / Module...`
    *   **Blue Card:** `Technical Concept / Skill...`
    *   Arrange them neatly in a 2x2 grid or a vertical list.

2.  **Select All Cards:**
    *   Click and drag a selection box around all four cards you just created.
    *   Alternatively, hold down the `Shift` key and click on each card one by one.

3.  **Create the Group:**
    *   With all four cards selected, **right-click** on one of the selected cards.
    *   From the context menu, choose **"Create group"**.

4.  **Name the Group:**
    *   You will now see a colored container drawn around your four cards, with a text field at the top that says "Group".
    *   **Double-click** on the word "Group".
    *   Type `LEGEND` and press Enter.

5.  **Customize the Group (Optional):**
    *   You can right-click the group's background (not the cards inside) to change the group's color if you wish.

**Outcome:** You now have a single, self-contained, movable object on your canvas that is your complete legend. This is the most organized and efficient way to prepare your thinking environment.
